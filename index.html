📖 "Our Government's Laws" (pp. 12–16)

Paragraph 1Every country has a government. A government is a group of people. This group of people works together to make important decisions for the country.
Paragraph 2In the United States, the government for the country is called the federal government. It runs national programs. Each state has its own government, too. Cities and towns also have their own local governments.
Paragraph 3All governments make laws. A law is a rule that everyone has to follow. A government makes laws to keep citizens safe.
Paragraph 4For example, every child in a car must wear a seat belt. That is the law. In most states, adults must wear seat belts too. Anyone who does not is breaking the law.
Paragraph 5Laws help the government keep order. Laws tell people what they can and cannot do. For example, there are laws about how fast a car can go. The speed limit on many highways is sixty-five miles per hour. There are also laws about which streets cars can drive down.
Paragraph 6Police officers work for the government. Some work for the local government. Others work for the state or federal government. They work to make sure people follow the laws.
Paragraph 7Many things can happen when a law gets broken. Here is an example. When a school bus has stopped to let students get on or off, cars are not allowed to pass a school bus that has stopped for students.
Paragraph 8But one day, a man drives past a school bus that has stopped for students. A police officer sees this. He makes the driver stop. The officer writes a ticket. The ticket says which law the driver broke, and it lists a fine the driver may have to pay.
Paragraph 9Sometimes, a person must go to court. In a courtroom, a judge is in charge. The judge knows all about laws. A jury is a group of people. They are chosen to listen to court cases. Many juries have twelve people. A judge and jury listen to see if the person broke the law.
Paragraph 10In court, a person tells his or her side of the story. Then, the jury decides if the person broke the law. Next, the judge decides what happens to the person.
Paragraph 11There are federal, state, and local governments. They all pass laws. The laws help keep people safe. The laws help keep order. Police officers help keep order too.
